Do you trust your model's output?

Superjective was built to find out by gathering authentic user preferences. In 2025 and 2026 it ran blind, side-by-side comparisons of frontier models: one prompt, several answers, the model names hidden until the user had chosen. The preferences affected model sampling for the user and were kept for analysis. The product shut down in October 2026. Some insights from its run are featured below.

AMLBench

Public benchmark, v0.2 (July 2026), Apache-2.0

Do AI agents clear money-laundering alerts the law requires them to escalate? AMLBench builds synthetic alerts with IBM's AMLSim and scores each escalation decision against deterministic Bank Secrecy Act rules, never an LLM judge, under adversarial prompts, operational pressure and deceptive cover stories drawn from real enforcement cases.

Deceptive cover stories lowered every model's sensitivity by 27 to 73 points, including the two models robust to everything else, and standard observability tooling raised no flags while it happened.

Superjective

Production system, 2025 to October 2026

A React and FastAPI application in which people compared anonymized responses from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, xAI and open-weight models, with a Thompson-sampling router that learned which models to show each person. Around it: a Chrome extension that drafted replies in any text field, with personal information stripped in the browser before anything was sent, and a phone line for comparisons by voice.

The preferences people recorded were benchmarked against Anthropic's HH-RLHF dataset.

Research along the way

The same question from the inside: whether a model's trustworthiness can be read off its internals before it shows up in its answers. Independent work, published on LessWrong.

  • 2B scoring model flags out-of-domain misalignment, suggesting specialist judges have potential for audits

    May 2026

    A Gemma-2-2B judge fine-tuned on insecure-code examples separated misaligned from secure-tuned Gemma-3-12B responses on out-of-domain safety prompts (Cohen's d = 0.29, p = 0.016). Tentative support for narrow, lightweight scorers as a primitive in model audits.

  • Emergent misalignment evident in activations at low poisoning doses, long before behavioral checks flag it

    April 2026

    Fine-tuning Llama 3.2-3B on mixes of misaligned data, activation drift reaches about 28% of its full-poisoning magnitude at a 5% dose, while behavioral evaluations show no signal until 50% or more.

  • How post-training shapes legal representations: probing SCOTUS opinions across model families

    March 2026

    Post-trained open models linearly represent key legal concepts from landmark Supreme Court opinions in the residual stream; base models mostly don't. Post-training appears to create geometric structure that pretraining alone leaves absent.
